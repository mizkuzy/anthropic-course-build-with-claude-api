## Hidden Gluten Checklist

- **Soy sauce:** use tamari labeled gluten-free, or coconut aminos
- **Sauces and dressings:** check for malt vinegar, wheat starch, or barley
- **Processed meats:** sausages, deli meats, and some marinated meats may contain wheat fillers
- **Seasoning blends and bouillon:** can contain wheat or barley
- **Oats:** only use certified gluten-free oats due to cross-contamination
- **Beer and malt beverages:** avoid, or choose certified GF beer
- **Protein powders and bars:** look for a gluten-free label, since many contain cookie or wafer ingredients
- **Medications and supplements:** some use wheat-based fillers, which is worth checking if you're sensitive

# Vegan Marathon Training Nutrition Plan

**Estimated needs** (assuming a moderately active adult male; adjust for age, sex, and training volume):

- **Calories:** roughly 2,800-3,200 kcal/day on high-mileage weeks, about 2,500-2,800 on lighter weeks
- **Carbohydrates:** 6-8 g/kg (about 400-550 g/day), highest on long-run days
- **Protein:** 1.6-2.0 g/kg (about 110-135 g/day), spread across meals
- **Fat:** about 20-30% of calories (roughly 70-90 g/day), mostly unsaturated

## Key Vegan Protein Sources
- Tofu, tempeh, seitan (highest protein per serving)
- Lentils, chickpeas, black beans, edamame
- Soy milk and soy yogurt (about 7-8 g protein per cup)
- Pea, soy, or rice protein powder
- Quinoa, oats, hemp seeds, pumpkin seeds

## Sample Day (about 3,000 kcal, ~470 g carbs, ~120 g protein)

**Breakfast: Overnight oats**
- 90 g rolled oats, 350 ml fortified soy milk, 1 scoop pea/soy protein
- 1 banana, handful of berries, 1 tbsp chia seeds, 1 tbsp maple syrup

**Mid-morning snack**
- Whole-grain bagel with 2 tbsp peanut butter and jam, or a smoothie with soy milk and fruit

**Lunch: Lentil and quinoa power bowl**
- 1.5 cups cooked quinoa, 1 cup cooked lentils
- Roasted sweet potato, spinach, roasted peppers
- Tahini-lemon dressing, pumpkin seeds

**Pre-run snack (1-2 hours before)**
- 2 rice cakes with jam, plus a banana (low in fiber and fat, so it sits well)

**Dinner: Tofu or tempeh stir-fry**
- 150 g firm tofu or tempeh, 1.5 cups cooked rice or noodles
- Broccoli, bell peppers, edamame, ginger-soy sauce

**Evening snack**
- Soy yogurt with granola and fruit, or a slice of whole-grain toast with hummus

## Fueling Around Training
- **Before long runs (2-3 hours out):** focus on easily digested carbs such as oats, bread, rice, or bananas, with moderate protein and low fat and fiber.
- **During runs over 60-90 minutes:** aim for 30-60 g carbs per hour from gels, chews, dates, maple syrup, or a sports drink. Practice this in training.
- **After runs (within about 60 minutes):** eat roughly 1 g carbs/kg plus 20-30 g protein, such as a soy smoothie with banana and protein powder, or a tofu rice bowl.
- **Hydration:** drink consistently through the day and replace sodium lost in sweat, especially on long or hot runs.

## Nutrients to Watch on a Vegan Diet
- **Vitamin B12:** essential. Take a reliable supplement (e.g., 25-100 µg daily or 2,000 µg weekly). Don't skip this.
- **Iron:** combine lentils, tofu, and chickpeas with vitamin C foods (citrus, peppers, berries) to improve absorption. Consider a blood test, since endurance athletes can become low in iron.
- **Calcium:** choose calcium-set tofu and fortified plant milks, and include leafy greens, tahini, and almonds.
- **Omega-3s:** include ground flaxseed, chia, walnuts, or an algae-based DHA/EPA supplement.
- **Vitamin D and iodine:** use fortified foods, iodized salt, or supplements as needed. Get levels checked if possible.
- **Zinc:** pumpkin seeds, lentils, and oats help, but some people need a supplement.

## Practical Tips
- Batch-cook grains, legumes, and tofu/tempeh for the week.
- Keep portable carb-rich snacks (bananas, dates, rice cakes, energy bars) in your bag.
- Adjust portions up on heavy training weeks and down during recovery or tapering.
- Monitor body weight, energy, and performance, and adjust intake if you're losing weight or feeling drained.

For a fully individualized plan, especially if you have health conditions, train at high volume, or want to optimize race performance, consider working with a sports dietitian who has experience with plant-based athletes. Getting baseline blood work (iron, B12, vitamin D) is also worthwhile.

# Nutrition Plan: Competitive Gymnast (150 cm, 45 kg, Tree-Nut Allergy)

This is an estimate. A sports dietitian or physician should adjust it based on age, sex, training load, and how the athlete's weight and energy trend over the next few weeks.

## Daily Targets (approximate)

- **Energy:** about 1,900–2,300 kcal/day. Gymnastics training often burns more than people expect, so eating enough matters more than cutting calories.
- **Protein:** about 75–90 g/day (roughly 1.6–2.0 g/kg), spread across 4–5 meals.
- **Carbohydrates:** about 230–300 g/day, the main fuel for training.
- **Fat:** about 55–70 g/day, from olive oil, avocado, seeds, dairy, eggs, and fish.
- **Fluids:** roughly 2–3 L/day, more on hard training days and in heat.
- **Micronutrients to watch:** iron, calcium, vitamin D, and zinc, which are commonly low in young female athletes.

## Sample Day (about 2,100 kcal)

**Breakfast (~550 kcal)**
- Oatmeal made with milk (1 cup cooked)
- Sliced banana and berries
- Sunflower seed butter (check label for nut-free processing)
- Optional: a glass of milk or fortified soy milk

**Mid-morning snack (~300 kcal)**
- Greek yogurt with honey
- Nut-free granola, if the label confirms it is free of tree nuts

**Lunch (~600 kcal)**
- Turkey, chicken, or tofu wrap in a whole-wheat tortilla
- Hummus, cheese, lettuce, tomato, and cucumber
- Apple and a glass of milk or fortified juice

**Pre-training snack, 60–90 min before (~200 kcal)**
- Rice cakes with jam, or a bagel with honey
- Easy to digest and low in fat and fiber, so it won't sit heavy during training

**Post-training dinner (~500–600 kcal)**
- Rice or quinoa bowl with salmon, lean beef, or tofu
- Roasted vegetables and a little olive oil or avocado
- Fruit or a small dessert for extra carbohydrates

**Evening snack (~150 kcal)**
- Cottage cheese with pineapple, or popcorn with a little cheese or butter

## Tree-Nut Safety Notes

- **Read labels every time.** Look for "may contain tree nuts" or "processed in a facility with tree nuts" warnings, and check granola, baked goods, chocolate, pesto (often pine nuts), and some cereals and protein bars.
- **Safe alternatives:** sunflower seed butter, pumpkin seeds, soy-based products, and oat or dairy milk. Avoid almond, cashew, and other nut milks.
- **Ask the allergist about coconut.** It is botanically a fruit and is not classified as a tree nut by the FDA, but some allergists advise caution, so check with the care team.
- **Carry prescribed epinephrine** (such as an auto-injector) if one has been prescribed, and make sure coaches and teammates know how to use it.

## Weight Stability During Season

- **Don't restrict.** Maintaining weight while training hard requires enough food. Undereating in gymnastics raises the risk of injury, illness, poor performance, and low energy availability (RED-S).
- **Track trends, not single weigh-ins.** Weight can fluctuate by 1–2 kg daily from hydration and food volume. Weekly averages are more useful.
- **Adjust gradually.** If weight drifts down, add about 200–300 kcal/day (for example, a snack or extra carbohydrate at training). If it rises beyond what's expected for growth and training, reduce portions slightly rather than cutting food groups.
- **Watch for warning signs:** persistent fatigue, frequent injuries or illness, loss of period (if applicable), mood changes, or obsessive thoughts about food or weight. These warrant prompt medical evaluation.

## Next Steps

For a competitive gymnast, especially a minor, it's worth working with a sports dietitian and a physician. They can check blood levels (ferritin, vitamin D), tailor the plan to the training schedule, and monitor growth and energy availability.
