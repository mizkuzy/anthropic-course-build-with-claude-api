## Adjusting for Training

- **Long-run and hard-session days:** Add 100-150 g carbs. Extra rice, bread, potatoes, fruit, or a sports drink works well.
- **Rest days:** Reduce carbs slightly and keep protein steady.
- **Pre-run (1-3 hours before):** Eat easily digestible carbs such as toast with jam, a banana, or rice cakes, with little fat or fiber.
- **During runs over 60-90 minutes:** Take 30-60 g carbs per hour from gels, dates, maple syrup packets, or sports drinks. Practice this in training, not on race day.
- **Post-run (within 60 minutes):** Have a carb-protein combination, such as a smoothie with soy milk, protein powder, and fruit.

## Protein Sources to Rotate

- Tofu, tempeh, seitan
- Lentils, chickpeas, black beans, split peas
- Soy milk, soy yogurt, edamame
- Pea or soy protein powder
- Quinoa, oats, hemp seeds, chia seeds, pumpkin seeds

## Nutrients to Watch

Vegan athletes are more likely to fall short on these, so consider:

- **Vitamin B12:** Supplement daily (25-100 mcg) or use fortified foods. This is essential.
- **Iron:** Pair plant iron (lentils, tofu, spinach, fortified cereals) with vitamin C foods like peppers, citrus, or tomatoes. Iron status is worth checking with a doctor, especially for endurance athletes.
- **Calcium:** Use fortified soy milk, calcium-set tofu, tahini, and leafy greens.
- **Omega-3s:** Include ground flaxseed, chia, walnuts, or an algae-based DHA/EPA supplement.
- **Vitamin D and iodine:** Consider supplements, especially in low-sunlight months. Iodized salt or seaweed also helps.
