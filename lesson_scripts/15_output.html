## Energy and macronutrient targets

At 150 cm and 45 kg (BMI about 20), the person is a healthy weight. To maintain weight while training, energy intake should roughly match total daily expenditure. For a lightly to moderately active adult woman, that's typically around 1,700 to 2,100 kcal per day, and somewhat more if training is intense or long.

- **Protein:** about 1.2 to 1.6 g/kg, roughly 55 to 70 g per day, spread across meals to support recovery.
- **Carbohydrates:** about 4 to 6 g/kg on training days, roughly 180 to 270 g. These are the main fuel for energy, so don't cut them.
- **Fats:** about 20 to 30% of calories, roughly 45 to 70 g, mostly unsaturated.
- **Iron, calcium, and vitamin D** deserve attention, especially for a lighter-bodied person who trains regularly. Iron deficiency is a common cause of low energy.

## Allergy-safe food choices

Avoid all tree nuts (including almond, walnut, cashew, pistachio, hazelnut, and their oils, butters, and "nut flours") and all shellfish (shrimp, crab, lobster, clams, mussels, oysters, scallops). Check labels for hidden sources such as pesto, some granolas, nougat, marzipan, and certain Asian sauces and stocks. Seeds (pumpkin, sunflower, chia, flax, hemp) and seed butters are good substitutes for nuts. Fish with fins (salmon, tuna, sardines) is a safe protein and omega-3 source.

## Practical tips for energy

- Eat every 3 to 4 hours so energy doesn't dip.
- Drink enough water; even mild dehydration causes fatigue. Aim for pale yellow urine.
- Include iron-rich foods such as lean red meat, lentils, tofu, fortified cereals, and spinach, pairing them with vitamin C foods to improve absorption.
- Get enough sleep; recovery and energy depend heavily on it.

Here's a practical plant-based fueling plan for a 178 cm, 70 kg marathon trainee. Exact needs vary with training volume, so treat the numbers as a starting point and adjust based on how you feel and how your weight trends.

## Daily Targets (approximate)

- **Calories:** ~2,800–3,300 kcal on moderate training weeks; higher (3,300–3,800+) during long-run and peak-mileage weeks
- **Carbohydrates:** 6–8 g/kg body weight, so roughly 420–560 g/day. Marathon training generally needs this high a share.
- **Protein:** 1.4–1.7 g/kg, so about 100–120 g/day. Plant proteins are slightly less digestible than animal proteins, so aim toward the upper end and spread intake across meals.
- **Fat:** ~20–30% of calories (roughly 70–100 g), mostly unsaturated
- **Fluids:** ~2–3 L/day baseline, plus fluid and sodium around training

## Key Nutrients to Watch

- **Vitamin B12:** Not reliably found in plant foods. Take a supplement (commonly 25–100 µg daily or 2,000 µg weekly) or eat fortified foods consistently.
- **Iron:** Plant iron is less absorbed. Include lentils, beans, tofu, fortified cereals, and pumpkin seeds, and pair them with vitamin C foods (citrus, peppers, tomatoes). Consider a blood test, since endurance athletes are prone to low iron.
- **Calcium:** Fortified plant milks, tofu made with calcium sulfate, leafy greens (kale, bok choy), and almonds. Aim for ~1,000 mg/day.
- **Vitamin D:** Supplementation is often needed, especially in winter or at higher latitudes.
- **Omega-3s:** Ground flaxseed, chia, walnuts, or an algae-based DHA/EPA supplement.
- **Iodine:** Use iodized salt or a supplement if you don't eat seaweed or use iodized salt.
- **Zinc:** Legumes, seeds, and whole grains; soaking and sprouting help absorption.

## Fueling Around Runs

- **Long runs (>75–90 min):** Take 30–60 g carbs per hour from gels, dates, sports drinks, or rice-syrup-based products (most are vegan; check labels for gelatin or honey).
- **Post-run (within ~60 min):** ~20–30 g protein plus carbs, such as a soy milk smoothie with fruit or a tofu rice bowl.
- **Race week:** Gradually increase carbs and reduce fiber and fat in the 24 hours before, and test everything in training first.

## Practical Tips

- Eat a larger share of carbs around training sessions and keep fats and very high-fiber foods away from pre-run meals.
- Batch-cook grains, beans, and tofu for the week.
- Track body weight and energy over 2–3 weeks; if weight drops steadily or performance fades, add calories.
